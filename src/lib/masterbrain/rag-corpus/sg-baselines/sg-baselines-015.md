At the shot level, an estimated 30–40% **of SG variance represents luck—uncontrollable factors** including bounce variations, wind gusts, and lie quality differences within nominally identical categories. The flagstick interaction exemplifies this dramatically: a chip that strikes the flagstick and drops yields +1.0 to +1.5 SG versus expectation; the identical chip that strikes the flagstick and stops 1 foot away yields −0.5 to −0.8 SG—a 2-stroke swing from centimeter-level outcome differences.

The AK Platform’s response is to implement shot quality metrics independent of outcome: Track- Man parameters (ball speed, launch angle, spin rate, curve consistency) provide process-based assessment that filters luck from skill evaluation. A player with excellent TrackMan approach metrics but poor SG:Approach over 10 rounds likely experienced negative luck; conversely, poor TrackMan metrics with good SG suggests positive luck unsustainable without technical improvement.

## 7.3 Strategic SG: Decision Quality Beyond Outcome

SG measures outcomes, not decisions—a fundamental limitation that the DECADE framework ad- dresses. A player who correctly lays up short of a water hazard (good decision) but hits a poor lay-up shot (bad outcome) receives negative SG for the lay-up, with no credit for the strategic avoidance of catastrophe. The AK Platform must integrate DECADE’s expected value calculations to provide **decision-adjusted SG: “Your SG on this hole was −0.3, but your strategic decision gained +0.2 in** expected value; the execution cost was −0.5.”

## 7.4 Mental Game Complementarity

SG captures no information about pre-shot routine consistency, pressure response, or error recovery patterns—factors that substantial sports psychology research identifies as performance differentiators. The AK Platform should integrate mental **game metrics (routine timing,** heart rate variability if available, post-error bounce-back rates) as complementary dimensions to SG-based skill assessment.
