The motor learning literature (Shea & Morgan’s contextual interference research, Wulf’s OPTIMAL
theory, Schmidt’s schema theory) suggests that putting’s high learnability stems from reduced degrees of
freedom and immediate feedback availability, while full-swing skills require more extensive motor program
development.

| Period | SG Display Characteristic | Training Emphasis | Coaching Message |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Base(SPES) | SG may not improve or may decline | Fundamental movement patterns, physical development, technique overhaul | “Building foundations for future SG gains” |
| Pre-competition | SG begins to stabilize and improve | Course simulation, pressure practice, strategy refinement | “Translating training into scoring” |
| Competition | SG as performance indicator | Maintenance, recovery, mental skills, tactical adjustment | “Managing energy, maximizing opportunity” |

The critical insight for platform implementation: during base periods, coaches must resist SG-driven
intervention when fundamental development is the priority. The platform should display foundational
skill metrics (TrackMan parameters, movement quality scores) alongside SG to prevent premature
optimization.
